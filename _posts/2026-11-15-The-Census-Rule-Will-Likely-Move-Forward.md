A few people, [including Mike Schneider who quoted me in his substack](https://open.substack.com/pub/countofcounting/p/hey-soon-to-be-democratic-majority ), have reached out to ask my opinion about how the [Census Bureau's proposed Residency Requirements regulation could be stopped.](https://handsoffourcens.us/)

While it's possible a [Congressional Review Act](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF10023) joint resolution prohibiting implementation is introduced in the next Congress and passes, it's unlikely to reach a veto proof majority. Given that Senator Murkowski was the [sole Republican to join Democrats in submitting a comment](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/library/files/peters-kim-colleagues-letter-to-census-bureau/ ) to the Bureau requesting the withdrawal of the rule, it would take quite a bit of wrangling even in the Senate where the bar is only to reach 10 or 11 Republicans. The lack of a rider prohibiting further consideration of the rule in the recent FY27 CJS appropriations bill and report is an indicator that this issue is not front of mind. In the House, 30ish of the remaining predominantly Hardline Republicans would need to join with all Democrats and that is a significantly higher bar.

In my opinion, the most likely path forward for preventing this rule from happening is: 1)  litigation under the [Administrative Procedures Act](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R41546) (there are lots of hooks into the APA, including arbitrary and capricious filings resulting from the fact that this was a significant rulemaking but only underwent less than 60 days notice, and it's likely that there will be insufficient "consideration" of significant comments) or directly on  grounds that the rulemaking deviates from the plain language of the Constitution (which, in a [Major Questions world](https://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4291030) would seem to be the ultimate bar for agencies not (re)interpreting Congressional intent); 2) appropriations riders in the next next fiscal year or continuing resolution (that would be FY28 possibly one in FY27 if they can land the plane during reconciliation); and, most likely, 3) a rapid reversal by the next Administration.

That latter option will be a rough one for Census career officials who will have to take extreme methodological measures in the 11th hour (not to mention redesigning and piloting forms). Still, I believe this Administration will finalize the rule pretty much as is - they might add back race and ethnicity just to provide critics with some carrot to demonstrate that they considered comments - while maintaining the proposed residency requirements preventing non-citizens from counting towards apportionment. And so, I think executive intervention by a more reasonable Administration in the future is the most likely option.

For the next steps, observers should watch the regulatory docket to see when the rule goes back to the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs for final review: [https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eoDetails?rrid=1442461](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eoDetails?rrid=1442461).
